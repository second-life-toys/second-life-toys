# The Field Guide

> Field guide - reference, not a how-to. To revive or use a toy, start with [Getting started](../getting-started.md) or the [Recovery matrix](../recovery-matrix.md).

I kept running into the same thing while I worked on this project. Someone would ask me a plain question about the toy, like "what is actually inside it," or "why does it go quiet after a few seconds," or "which firmware am I even looking at," and the answer had nowhere to live. The how-to guides tell you what to tap. They do not stop to explain what the toy *is*. So I started writing it all down, and that is what the field guide is.

## What the field guide is (reference, not a how-to)

Think of this as the encyclopedia for the Sphero Spider-Man toy. It describes what the toy is made of, how it behaves, what the game was, how to read the little signals it gives off, and where it fits in the history of these figures. It is the reference layer, the stuff you look up, not the stuff you follow step by step.

Every page here opens with the same one-line banner you see up top, and that is on purpose. The field guide is for understanding the toy. It is not the place to come when your toy is stuck and you just want it working again. For that, I point you at the how-to docs, and I do that a lot on this page.

## How it differs from the user docs

The difference is simple. The user docs are tasks. The field guide is facts.

- The **user docs** ([Getting started](../getting-started.md), the [Recovery matrix](../recovery-matrix.md), [Troubleshooting](../troubleshooting.md)) tell an owner how to revive a toy and how to use one. They are written to be followed top to bottom, with a screenshot for every screen.
- The **field guide** tells you what the toy is and how it behaves. It is written to be browsed and searched, not followed. Nothing here is a step you carry out on your toy.

Frankly, if you only have five minutes and a dead toy, the field guide is not where you should be. Come back to it later when the toy is working and you get curious about the thing sitting on your shelf.

## Scope and what we deliberately leave out

I want to be clear about where this stops, because it matters.

The field guide covers consumer and museum facts only. That means what the toy is, what it does, what state it can be in, and what its history was. It is the kind of thing you would want to know as an owner, or as someone preserving one of these figures for the record.

It does **not** cover privileged access, low-level protocol detail, keys, the inner workings of the replacement cloud, or anything that could identify a specific unit or its owner. Naturally the intake log is aggregate and anonymized, counts only, with no serial numbers, no hardware addresses, no account or profile IDs, and no real owner's hero name. This is a repair and preservation project, and a big part of doing that responsibly is knowing which facts belong in public and which do not.

And to say it plainly, reviving or using a toy is not covered here at all. That lives in [Getting started](../getting-started.md) and the [Recovery matrix](../recovery-matrix.md), on purpose. The field guide describes the toy. The how-to docs act on it.

## Where owners should start instead

If you landed here because your toy is dead, stuck, or saying "grab the app" forever, you are one click from the right place. Do not read the field guide first.

- **Just want to fix your toy?** Go to [Getting started](../getting-started.md). It gets the free app on your phone and walks the whole rescue with a screenshot of every screen.
- **Want to know if your exact situation is recoverable?** The [Recovery matrix](../recovery-matrix.md) has a plain-language row for each toy state and an honest status on how sure we are.
- **A scan or connection is not working?** [Troubleshooting](../troubleshooting.md) covers the common gotchas, and most of them are one small thing.

In the end, the field guide is here for after the rescue, when the toy is back on your shelf and you want to understand it. Fix the toy first, then come read about it.
